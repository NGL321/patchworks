# Citation pass: information cohomology (patchworks#15)

Serves patchworks#15, "Where the geometry lives: manifolds, charts, and projections." Per the
map's house rule, **citations validate design; they never seed it.** Nothing below is a proposal.
This note reports what the literature establishes, and — more importantly for this ticket — what
it does not.

All degree statements were read from the primary text (the Baudot–Bennequin PDF and Vigneaux's
published paper), not from summaries. A user recollection was supplied for checking; **it is
partly wrong, and the corrections are stated explicitly in §2.**

## 1. The citation, confirmed

**Baudot, P. & Bennequin, D. (2015). "The Homological Nature of Entropy." *Entropy* 17(5),
3253–3318.** doi:10.3390/e17053253.

The recollection of the reference is exact — title, authors, journal, volume, issue, year all
check out. Page range is 3253–3318.

## 2. What actually sits in which degree

The recollection under test was: `H⁰ = "feature kernel"`, `H¹ = entropy`, `H² = mutual
information`. **One of those three is right.** Here is the actual indexing.

### `C⁰` and `H⁰` — constants, not a kernel

Baudot & Bennequin define an information `N`-cochain as a family of measurable functions of a
probability law `P`, indexed by sequences `(S₁; …; S_N)` of random variables, whose values depend
only on the image law `(S₁,…,S_N)∗P` (a condition they explicitly call **"a 'locality'
condition"**). Then, verbatim:

> "For `N = 0` this gives only the constants."

And the computation, verbatim (their §2, `m = 0` case): a 0-cochain must be constant because
`1 ∈ S` and `Q₁` has one element; every constant is a cocycle since `δf(X₀; P) = X₀.f(P) − f(P) =
f(1)(1 − 1) = 0`. Their conclusion:

> "Consequently `H⁰` is `R`. This corresponds to the hypothesis `1 ∈ S`, meaning connexity of the
> category. If `m` components exist, we recover them in the same way and `H⁰` is isomorphic to
> `Rᵐ`."

Vigneaux reproduces this independently in his §4.4 ("Determination of `H⁰`"): `H⁰(S, F₁(Q)) ≅ R`
for Shannon (`α = 1`), and `H⁰(S, F_α(Q)) ≅ ⟨0⟩` for Tsallis `α ≠ 1`. His §4.7 gives the
interpretation: **`H⁰` is "global sections of `N` that are invariant under the action of `A`"** —
"the only sections are constant functionals."

> **Correction.** `H⁰` is the space of **constants** — one dimension per connected component of
> the information structure. It is the invariant/trivial part, exactly as the ticket brief
> guessed. It is **not** a "feature kernel," and nothing in either paper attaches that phrase or
> anything like it to degree 0. The phrase most likely leaked in from the *sheaf-cohomology* side
> of the vocabulary, where `H⁰` genuinely is a kernel (`ker δ⁰`, the global sections of a cellular
> sheaf). That is a different theory — see §3.

### `H¹` — entropy, and the chain rule as the cocycle condition. Correct.

Baudot & Bennequin, verbatim: "The fact that classical entropy `H(X; P) = −Σᵢ pᵢ log₂ pᵢ` is a
1-co-cycle is the fundamental equation `H(X, Y) = H(X) + X.H(Y)`" — where `X.` is Shannon's
averaged conditioning, `S₀.F(…;P) = Σⱼ P(S₀ = vⱼ)·F(…; P|S₀ = vⱼ)`, their Eq. (1). So **the chain
rule is literally the 1-cocycle equation.** Their headline result:

> **Theorem A.** "For the full simplex `Δ(Ω)`, and if `S` is the monoid generated by a set of at
> least two variables, such that each pair takes at least four values, then the information
> co-homology space of degree one is one-dimensional and generated by the classical entropy."

Note the hypotheses; they are not decoration (§4). Vigneaux's summary of the same fact is the
cleanest one-liner available: "Shannon entropy is to some extent analogous to the fundamental
class of an orientable connected manifold: a generator of the one-dimensional `H¹(S, F(Q))` for a
strongly connected pair `(S, Q)`."

> **Confirmed as recollected.** `H¹` is generated by Shannon entropy, and the chain rule *is* the
> 1-cocycle condition. This half of the recollection is exactly right.

### Mutual information — a **coboundary**, therefore **zero in cohomology**

This is the load-bearing correction, and it cuts against `H² = mutual information` in the strongest
possible way.

Baudot & Bennequin define the higher mutual informations after Hu Kuo Ting as the alternating sums
`I_N(S₁;…;S_N; P) = Σ_{k=1..N} (−1)^{k−1} Σ_{|I|=k} H(S_I; P)`, with `I₁ = H` and `I₂(S;T) = H(S) +
H(T) − H(S,T)` the ordinary mutual information. Then, their **Theorem B**:

> `I_{2m} = δ_t δ δ_t … δ δ_t H`, `I_{2m+1} = −δ δ_t δ δ_t … δ δ_t H`
>
> "Thus odd information quantities are information co-cycles, because they are in the image of `δ`,
> and even information quantities are twisted (or topological) co-cycles, because they are in the
> image of `δ_t`."

and in §3, stated flatly:

> "We deduce from here that higher mutual informations are **co-boundaries** for `δ` or `δ_t`
> according that their order is odd or even respectively."

The simplest instance is `I₂ = δ_t H`.

> **Correction, and it is not a quibble.** `I₂` is a 2-**cochain** satisfying a cocycle condition,
> but it is the image of `H` under a coboundary operator. **A coboundary is zero in cohomology by
> definition.** So mutual information is emphatically *not* a nontrivial class of `H²`; it is a
> canonical *representative built from the degree-1 class*, and its cohomology class vanishes. The
> whole content of Theorem B is that the entire tower `I₂, I₃, I₄, …` is generated by repeatedly
> applying `δ` and `δ_t` to the single degree-1 generator, entropy. There is nothing new in higher
> degrees; there is one generator and a tower of its coboundaries.
>
> Two operators are in play and they must not be conflated: `δ` (the non-homogeneous bar
> coboundary, whose first term carries the **conditioning** action `S₀.F`) and `δ_t` ("twisted" or
> "topological," identical but with the conditioning action replaced by the trivial one, `Y.F =
> F`). `I_N` is a coboundary for `δ` when `N` is odd and for `δ_t` when `N` is even. Any claim that
> "`H²` = mutual information" collapses the two.

### What *is* in higher degrees: an open problem

Immediately after Theorem A, verbatim:

> **"Problem 1. Compute the homology of higher degrees.**
> We conjecture that for binary variables it is zero, but that in general non-trivial classes
> appear, deduced from polylogarithms."

So as of the founding paper, degrees ≥ 2 are **unknown, conjecturally trivial in the binary case**,
and if non-trivial expected to involve polylogarithms and motivic machinery (Cathelineau, Dupont,
Bloch, Goncharov, Elbaz-Vincent, Gangl) — not information quantities anyone measures. Vigneaux
adds only a speculation, not a theorem: for a simplicial structure `(K, M)`, "higher cohomology
groups **might** be linked to the higher Betti numbers" (emphasis added; his word is "might").
Vigneaux's §4.7 gives `H²` a Hochschild-style interpretation as classifying **extensions** — again,
not mutual information.

**Summary table, corrected against the primary sources:**

| Degree | What is actually there | Recollection under test | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `H⁰` | Constants; `R` per connected component (`Rᵐ`). Invariant global sections. | "feature kernel" | **Wrong** — it is constants/invariants |
| `H¹` | One-dimensional, generated by Shannon entropy; chain rule = 1-cocycle condition | entropy | **Correct** |
| `H²` and up | Open problem (BB Problem 1); conjecturally zero for binary variables. `H²` interprets as extensions. | mutual information | **Wrong** — `I_N` are *coboundaries*, hence **zero** in cohomology |

## 3. The site — and why this `H¹` is not the sheaf's `H¹`

**The site of information cohomology.** An *information structure* `S` is a set of random
variables on `Ω`, identified when they induce the same partition, ordered by refinement (`Y ≤ Z`
when `Z` refines `Y`), and closed under **join** — "the less fine partition that is finer than `Y`
and `Z`." Baudot & Bennequin: "This defines a **monoid structure** on the set `Π(Ω)` of partitions
of `Ω`, with `1` as a unit, and where each element is idempotent." So the base object is a
**poset/semilattice of partitions (contexts) of one sample space**, with joint measurement as the
multiplication. Coefficients are **probabilistic functionals** — measurable functions of a
probability law `P` ranging over a conditioning-stable simplicial subcomplex `P ⊆ Δ(Ω)` — and the
differential is the **non-homogeneous bar coboundary** of that monoid acting by **conditioning**.

Vigneaux makes the site explicit and formal: each information structure "can be regarded as a
**ringed site with trivial topology**; the structure ring is generated by the observables
themselves and its multiplication corresponds to **joint measurement**," and information cohomology
is "**a derived functor** in the category of modules over the structure ring," with the bar
construction furnishing a projective resolution. Trivial topology — every presheaf is a sheaf —
so the "sheaf" language here does no covering-family work at all; it is Hochschild-type cohomology
of an algebra acting by conditioning.

**The site of cellular sheaf cohomology** (Hansen & Ghrist, already cited in
`docs/research/019-tick-semantics-citations.md`) is a different thing in every slot. The base is a
**cell complex — for Patchworks, a graph**: cells are vertices and edges of the actual
communication topology, ordered by face incidence. Coefficients are **vector spaces assigned to
cells** (node stalks, communication lanes) with **restriction maps** `F_{v⊴e}` along incidences.
The differential is the **cellular coboundary** `δ⁰`, and `H⁰(X; F) ≅ Γ(X; F)`, the **global
sections** — Hansen & Ghrist state this isomorphism directly — i.e. `ker δ⁰`, the assignments on
which every pair of neighbours already agrees. That is exactly the zero-disagreement subspace
`01-cell-and-sheaf.md` is built around.

**Verdict on the framing question: confirmed — these are unrelated objects sharing notation.**
Line them up:

| | Information cohomology | Cellular sheaf cohomology on a graph |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Poset/monoid of **partitions of one sample space**, ordered by refinement, closed under join | **Cell complex / graph**: vertices and edges, ordered by face incidence |
| Topology on the site | **Trivial** (every presheaf is a sheaf) | Cellular / Alexandrov on the face poset |
| Coefficients | **Functionals of a probability law**, `P ∈ P ⊆ Δ(Ω)` | **Vector spaces per cell** (node stalk `n`, communication lane `m`) |
| Differential | Bar coboundary with a **conditioning** action `S₀.F = Σ P(S₀=v)F(·|S₀=v)` | Cellular coboundary via **linear restriction maps** `F_{v⊴e}` |
| `H⁰` | **Constants** `Rᵐ` | **Global sections** `Γ(X;F) = ker δ⁰` — consistent assignments |
| `H¹` | **Shannon entropy**, one-dimensional | Obstruction to extending local sections; `dim` reflects graph + stalk structure |
| Depends on the communication graph? | **No.** There is no graph. | **Yes.** It *is* the graph. |

The two theories share the letters `H`, `δ`, "cochain," and "sheaf," and share nothing else. There
is no functor, no comparison map, and no spectral sequence in the literature relating a Patchworks
lane `H¹` to an entropy class. **Any Patchworks document that puts the two `H¹`s in the same
sentence must say which is meant.** In particular, `01-cell-and-sheaf.md`'s Dirichlet-energy/
harmonic-space machinery lives entirely on the *cellular* side; information cohomology contributes
nothing to it.

One nuance worth keeping straight rather than exploiting: Baudot & Bennequin call their cochain
condition — that a cochain's value depend only on the image law `(S₁,…,S_N)∗P` — "a 'locality'
condition," and Vigneaux says this "is the appropriate notion of locality and justifies the
introduction of presheaves." That is **not** Patchworks' *graph-locality* or *cell-locality*
(`CONTEXT.md` defines both). Theirs is a naturality/factorization condition on a functional's
dependence on a distribution; Patchworks' is a statement about which cells may exchange or read.
The word coincides; the concept does not.

## 4. Vigneaux: what he adds, and the standing of the theory

**Primary reference.** Vigneaux, J.P. (2020). "Information structures and their cohomology."
*Theory and Applications of Categories* **35**(38), 1476–1529. arXiv:1709.07807 (v4, Nov 2021).
Thesis: *Topology of statistical systems: a cohomological approach to information theory*,
Université de Paris, 2019.

What he adds, from the paper itself:

1. **Foundational rebuild.** He introduces the *category* of information structures and shows each
   is a ringed site with trivial topology, then defines information cohomology as a **derived
   functor** `Extⁿ(O, −)` rather than by a hand-written cochain complex — and proves the bar
   construction gives a projective resolution, thereby *recovering* Baudot–Bennequin's explicit
   complex as a computation rather than a definition. This is the step that makes the theory
   standard homological algebra instead of a bespoke construction.
2. **Sharper `H¹`, with the degeneracies found.** His Theorem 1.3.2 determines `H¹` under stated
   non-degeneracy hypotheses: `H¹(S, F₁(Q)) ≅ R^{π₀(S*)}` for Shannon, `R^{π₀(S*)}/Δ` for Tsallis
   `α ≠ 1`. He also proves the **failure** cases, which Baudot & Bennequin's Theorem A hypotheses
   ("at least two variables, each pair takes at least four values") gestured at without
   delimiting: **"When some of the minimal objects of `S` is irreducible (it cannot be written as a
   nontrivial product), the group `H¹(S, F_α(Q))` has infinite dimension"** (his Prop. 4.6.3), and
   he works through further "pathological examples." So *the celebrated one-dimensionality of `H¹`
   is conditional*, and the conditions are structural properties of the information structure.
3. **Tsallis as the same phenomenon.** The `α`-parameterized coefficient family yields Shannon at
   `α = 1` and Tsallis `α`-entropy otherwise, both characterized as the unique 1-cocycles.
4. **Degree interpretations** (his §4.7, after Hochschild): `H⁰` = invariant sections, `H¹` =
   crossed homomorphisms modulo principal ones, `H²` = extensions.

**Standing: niche but sound — not contested.** The theory is published in respectable venues
(*Entropy*; *Theory and Applications of Categories*), has an independent foundational reconstruction
by a second author (Vigneaux) that *corrects and delimits* rather than overturns, and has attracted
further independent work — a Toronto thesis on its structure (Hubert Dubé, "On the Structure of
Information Cohomology"), a homological characterization of generalized multinomial coefficients
(arXiv:2003.02021), extension to vector-valued observables (arXiv:2107.04377), and Manin &
Marcolli's use of it inside a larger categorical model of neural information networks
(arXiv:2006.15136, published in *Compositionality*). No refutation or serious dispute surfaced.
Equally, there is no large community: the citation graph is small and heavily self-referential
among a handful of authors. **Read it as: correct, cared for by few, and with its central open
problem (higher degrees) still open twelve years on.**

## 5. Applicability — the decisive question

> **Has anyone used information cohomology as a training objective or a local learning rule for a
> neural network or GNN?**
>
> **No. Nobody has done this.** Searches across the topic returned no such work; the closest
> claims by the theory's own authors are explicitly analysis and explicitly preliminary. This is
> the honest answer the ticket asked for.

What exists, ordered by how close it gets:

- **Baudot's own work is analysis, and says so.** "The Poincaré–Shannon Machine: Statistical
  Physics and Machine Learning Aspects of Information Cohomology" (*Entropy* 21(9), 881, 2019)
  applies the machinery to **unsupervised classification of gene expression modules and cell
  types** — the "machine learning" is clustering/condensation as an *interpretation* ("makes two
  phenomena coincide, i.e., condensation in statistical physics and clustering of data points"),
  not gradient training. Where the paper reaches toward architecture, it proposes "unsupervised
  deep learning where the neural network architecture is given by the chain complex" and leaves
  supervised application **"for further work."** That further work has not appeared. Similarly
  "Topological Information Data Analysis" (*Entropy* 21(9), 869, 2019; arXiv:1907.04242) is a
  **data-analysis methodology**, demonstrated on 41 genes.
- **`INFOTOPO`** (Baudot, github.com/pierrebaudot/infotopopy) is a working Python package that
  estimates entropies and multivariate mutual informations over the simplicial lattice. It is an
  **estimator and visualizer**, not a differentiable layer and not an optimizer.
- **Manin & Marcolli** (arXiv:2006.15136, *Compositionality*) give "a functorial assignment to
  networks of corresponding information structures and information cohomology, and a cohomological
  version of integrated information," plus a categorical form of Hopfield dynamics. This is a
  **structural/theoretical model of networks**. No training objective, no experiments, no code.

Adjacent work that *does* train things, and is the real comparison class for a cell-local rule:

- **Infomorphic networks — the closest live thing to what Patchworks#5 wants.** Makkeh, Graetz,
  Schneider, Ehrlich, Priesemann & Wibral, "A general framework for interpretable neural learning
  based on local information-theoretic goal functions," ***PNAS* 122(10), 2025**
  (arXiv:2306.02149). They derive **"a corresponding parametric local learning rule"** from
  **Partial Information Decomposition**, giving each neuron its own information-theoretic goal
  function, and demonstrate supervised, unsupervised, and memory tasks. **This is a real,
  published, per-neuron local learning rule derived from an information-theoretic decomposition —
  and it uses PID, not cohomology.** It contains no cohomology at all. If Patchworks wants a
  citation for "a cell-local learning rule shaped by an information-theoretic quantity," this is
  the paper that actually exists; information cohomology is not.
- **PID itself** (Williams & Beer) supplies the unique/redundant/synergistic decomposition that
  work rests on. Note that Baudot & Bennequin's `I₃` minima are interpreted as Borromean links and
  "synergy," so the two literatures are looking at the same phenomena — but PID is the one that
  has been made into an objective.
- **Information bottleneck and infomax** are the long-standing information-theoretic training
  objectives. Both are ordinary variational objectives over mutual information estimates; neither
  is cohomological, and neither needs to be.

**Bearing on the Patchworks decision (`load-bearing formalism` vs `interpretive lens`).** The
literature supports **interpretive lens only**, and does so unambiguously. Three independent
reasons, each sufficient on its own:

1. **There is no precedent.** Zero prior systems train anything by a cohomological information
   objective. `01-cell-and-sheaf.md` already carries one bet with no literature behind it (the
   shared frozen body, with a named falsification test). Adding a second unprecedented bet in the
   same architecture, in a formalism whose central open problem is unsolved, is a different order
   of risk from the first.
2. **The mathematics does not offer a gradient.** `H¹` is a **one-dimensional** vector space whose
   generator is entropy. The theorem's content is a **uniqueness/characterization** statement —
   "entropy is the only thing that satisfies the chain rule with this locality condition" — not a
   variational principle. There is no quantity here to descend on that is not simply entropy, and
   descending on entropy is infomax under a longer name. And the natural candidate for "higher
   structure to optimize," the mutual-information tower, is by Theorem B **a tower of coboundaries
   of the one generator** — cohomologically zero and carrying no information the entropy class did
   not already carry.
3. **It is over the wrong site.** Per §3, the theory has no graph in it. Patchworks' locality is
   graph-locality and cell-locality; information cohomology's "locality" is a naturality condition
   on distributions. Nothing in the theory sees the edge structure a Patchworks cell is confined
   to, so it cannot by itself say what a *cell* may compute.

Where it **is** legitimately usable: as a post-hoc **analysis** of a trained Patchworks graph —
computing `I_k` over small sets of node-stalk features to ask whether a cell's chart carries
synergistic structure — and as a **vocabulary check**, since it is the sharpest available statement
of *why* entropy and the chain rule are canonical rather than conventional. Both are interpretive.

## 6. Cost

Prohibitive at any interesting width, and it scales with **the number of random variables, not the
number of samples**.

- **Full partition lattice: doubly exponential.** Baudot reports the general case as
  `O(exp(exp(Nⁿ)))` for `n` `N`-ary variables — concretely, for 16 variables taking 8 values each,
  "the partition lattice of variables exhibits around `e^(e^248) − 1 ≥ 2^200` elements to compute."
  This is not a large constant; it is unusable.
- **Simplicial sub-lattice (what `INFOTOPO` actually computes): `O(2ⁿ)`.** The restriction to the
  simplicial sub-lattice — all subsets of the `n` variables — evaluates `C(n,k)` terms at each
  degree `k`, summing to `2ⁿ`.
- **Measured ceiling: `n ≈ 21` variables.** Baudot states plainly that "the available computational
  power restricted the analysis to a maximum of `n = 21` variables (or 21 dimensions)," with the
  `INFOTOPO` documentation reporting ~21 variables in roughly 3 hours on an Intel Core i7-4910MQ.
- **A second, independent wall: sampling.** Estimating joint entropies over `k` variables needs
  enough samples to populate a joint histogram whose support grows like `Nᵏ`. Baudot flags this as
  its own insurmountable restriction. A GPU does not help here at all — this is a statistics
  problem, not an arithmetic-throughput problem.

**Against Patchworks' single-consumer-GPU constraint:** the `2ⁿ` is in the *number of variables*,
so a single cell with a node stalk of dimension `n` (`CONTEXT.md`'s `n`, treated as `n` variables)
is already at or past the measured ceiling, before any question of doing this per cell, per tick,
or inside a training loop. The workload is also combinatorial enumeration plus histogramming, which
is exactly the shape that does **not** batch — directly against `01-cell-and-sheaf.md`'s strongest
compute argument, that "the whole graph's inference phase is a single batched evaluation." Even as
offline analysis, it fits only over a hand-picked handful of features.

## 7. What the literature supports and does not support

**Supports:**
- Entropy is canonical, not conventional: it is *the* generator of a one-dimensional `H¹`, and the
  chain rule *is* the cocycle condition. (BB Thm A; Vigneaux Thm 1.3.2, under stated hypotheses.)
- The theory is mathematically sound and in good standing, if small.
- A cell-local learning rule derived from an information-theoretic decomposition is a real,
  published, working thing — via **PID / infomorphic networks (PNAS 2025)**, not via cohomology.

**Does not support:**
- `H⁰` as a "feature kernel." `H⁰` is constants (`Rᵐ`).
- `H² =` mutual information. Every `I_N` for `N ≥ 2` is a **coboundary**, hence **zero** in
  cohomology; the whole tower is generated from the single degree-1 class.
- Any reading in which higher degrees hold richer information structure. Higher degrees are BB's
  **Problem 1**, still open, conjecturally trivial for binary variables.
- Any identification, or even relation, between information cohomology's `H¹` and the `H¹` of a
  cellular sheaf on the Patchworks graph. Different site, different coefficients, different
  differential, no comparison map in the literature.
- Information cohomology as a training objective or a local learning rule. **No prior work
  whatsoever.** The theory's own authors leave it as future work and have not returned to it.
- Any use of it at Patchworks' scale on one GPU. `O(2ⁿ)` in the number of variables, measured
  ceiling `n ≈ 21`, plus an independent sampling wall.

**Net recommendation for patchworks#15, stated as the ticket framed the choice:** information
cohomology enters as an **interpretive lens**, not as load-bearing formalism. It is the right
citation for *why entropy and the chain rule are canonical*, and the wrong object for *where the
geometry lives* — the geometry the spec actually runs on is the cellular sheaf over the graph, and
that is a different theory that Patchworks already cites correctly
(`docs/research/019-tick-semantics-citations.md`). If a cell-local, information-theoretic learning
rule is wanted for patchworks#5, the live literature is **PID / infomorphic networks**.
