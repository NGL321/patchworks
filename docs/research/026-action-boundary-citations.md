# Citation pass: action and the boundary (patchworks#26)

Validates the design closed in patchworks#6 (`docs/spec/04-action-and-the-boundary.md`,
`docs/adr/0003-action-is-prediction-the-world-clears.md`). Citations validate after the fact per
the map's Notes; this document does not revise the closed design — it only flags where a source
threatens a claim already made. Vocabulary follows `CONTEXT.md`: Patchworks' side of every
comparison is described in its own terms (chart, node stalk, communication lane, disagreement,
reconciliation, restriction map, boundary cell, sensory edge, motor edge, efference copy,
sensorimotor rim, relay cell, cell contract); the prior art's side is described in its own field's
terms. Where a source could not be reached, that is stated rather than papered over.

## Headline verdict, stated plainly

**The generative/action-selection opposition in the literature is directional in the free-energy
mathematics Patchworks' identity descends from, and structural in the specific framework the spec
names itself after.** These are two different primary sources answering two different questions,
and they disagree:

- **Directional — supports the spec.** Friston, Daunizeau, Kilner & Kiebel (2010), "Action and
  behavior: a free-energy formulation," derives action and perception from **one scalar free-energy
  functional `F`** over **one generative model** (their Eq. 8: "the generative model has no notion
  of action; it just produces predictions that action tries to fulfil"). Their Eq. 9 is a set of
  coupled gradient-descent equations — `μ̇x`, `μ̇v`, `μ̈θ`, `ȧ` — that differ only in *which variable*
  the same free-energy gradient is taken with respect to; action and perception are two partial
  derivatives of the same `F`, not two objects. Verbatim: "action must suppress sensory prediction
  errors... Equation 9 embodies a nice convergence of action and perception: perception tries to
  suppress prediction error by adjusting expectations... while action tries to fulfil these
  predictions by changing those signals" (p. 234). This is the mathematics behind the spec's own
  chosen identity — action is prediction the world clears — and it is directional exactly as the
  spec bets: the opposition is a property of which variable a gradient targets (hidden state vs.
  motor plant), not a property of two separate populations.
- **Structural — contradicts the spec's rhetorical premise.** Rao, Gklezakos & Sathish (2022/2024),
  "Active Predictive Coding: A Unified Neural Framework for Learning Hierarchical World Models for
  Perception and Planning" (arXiv:2210.13461; published *Neural Computation* 36(1), 2024) — the
  paper that gives the framework its name, and the one the ticket flags as "very likely THE key
  source" — is explicitly **paired networks per level**: "each level employing a state network and
  an action network" (§4), generated by two separate hypernetworks `Hˢ` and `Hᵃ` producing two
  separate RNNs `fˢ` and `fᵃ` (Fig. 1A). This is precisely the "two paired populations per site"
  alternative ADR-0003 names and rejects. It is not a loose architectural habit — the paper's own
  Fig. 1 caption calls out the pairing as the load-bearing move, and planning (§6.2) is done by
  **explicit rollout**: "random state-action trajectories of length 4 were generated using `Fs`...
  the action sequence with the highest total reward was selected and its first action was executed"
  — random-shooting model-predictive control, i.e., genuine simulation of counterfactual futures,
  not clamp propagation.

**One-sentence reasoning:** the spec's identity ("action is prediction the world clears") is
directly supported by the foundational free-energy mathematics it is drawn from, but the spec's
opening rhetorical move — invoking "active predictive coding" as if it already names one opposition
running in two directions — mischaracterizes the one paper actually called that, which is
structurally two-sided at every level it has. The spec survives on the mathematics it draws on; it
does not survive as a redescription of Rao's own APC.

This is not treated as a violation requiring revision, per the map's Notes (citations validate
after the fact rather than re-litigating a closed design) — but it is a real and precisely
locatable place where the spec's framing oversells its relationship to its own namesake, and it is
recorded under Revision tickets below alongside the planning-without-rollout issue it entails (see
"Planning without rollout").

## 1. Action as fulfilled prediction, and its documented lookahead limit

### Source

- Friston, K., Daunizeau, J., Kilner, J., Kiebel, S.J. (2010). "Action and behavior: a free-energy
  formulation." *Biological Cybernetics* 102(3), 227–260. Read in full (PDF via
  fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk).
- Modarres Mousavi, S. et al. / cited survey: "Modeling motor control in continuous-time Active
  Inference: a survey," arXiv:2310.05144 (v1, read via HTML rendering).
- Parr, T., Pezzulo, G., Friston, K. (2022). *Active Inference: The Free Energy Principle in Mind,
  Brain, and Behavior*. MIT Press — cited by the survey above as the source of discrete/hierarchical
  active inference with expected free energy over policies; not independently read (see gaps).

### Findings

Friston et al. (2010) derive both perception and action as descent on the same free-energy bound
`F(s̃, μ)`. §2.3, Eq. 6: `μ(t)* = argmin_μ F(s̃(t), μ(t))` and `a(t)* = argmin_a F(s̃(t), μ(t))` —
literally the same functional minimized with respect to two different variables. §3, Eq. 8: "the
generative model has no notion of action; it just produces predictions that action tries to
fulfil" — action is defined as *whatever changes the world so a standing prediction is discharged*,
not as a separately modelled process. §3, Eq. 9's fourth line, `ȧ = −∂F/∂a ≈ −∂ε̃ᵀ/∂a · ξ_s`, makes
this concrete: action descends the same precision-weighted prediction-error gradient perception
does, restricted to the sensory (here proprioceptive) prediction error `ε̃_s` a motor plant can
actually discharge. §3.1: "The free-energy formulation offers a simpler view; prediction errors are
not there to finesse motor signals; **they are the motor signals**." This is the identity the spec
adopts near-verbatim.

The paper's own worked examples are exclusively reflexive/continuous: retinal stabilization
(oculomotor smooth pursuit), cued reaching, and goal-directed reaching via the mountain-car
problem — single continuous trajectories toward a standing attractor, with no explicit comparison
of discrete alternative courses of action anywhere in the paper. It does not itself claim to handle
multi-step deliberative planning, and does not discuss the limitation explicitly — this 2010 paper
is silent on lookahead rather than flagging it as a known gap.

The documented limitation is instead found in the literature that grew up around this formulation
to address exactly that silence. The 2310.05144 survey states it plainly: "Discrete models are
critical to perform planning and decision-making, but handling continuous signals is key when
interacting with the external environment," and characterizes continuous active inference's motor
control as arising from "low-level suppression of prediction errors" — sufficient for reactive
reaching, but not a mechanism that "can intrinsically generate multi-step sequences." The survey
cites Parr, Pezzulo & Friston's *Active Inference* monograph as the source that introduces
discrete-time active inference with expected free energy over policies specifically to close this
gap — i.e., the field's own response to the reflexive formulation's lookahead limit was to build a
second, structurally distinct discrete/policy layer on top of it (see "Planning without rollout"
below), not to extend the continuous formulation itself.

### Correspondences

- Friston's `F(s̃, μ)` minimized by both `μ` and `a` is the mathematical backbone for the spec's "one
  algorithm running in opposite directions" — but Patchworks' object is finer-grained than Friston's:
  Patchworks locates the opposition in the **edge** (sensory edge vs. motor edge), where Friston
  locates it in **which variable of one scalar** a gradient is taken with respect to (hidden state
  `μ` vs. plant-affecting action `a`). These are compatible readings — an edge in Patchworks
  corresponds to a subset of the `s̃` a cell's prediction is compared against — but the spec's
  "disagreement is cleared by the world moving" has no literal analogue in Friston's math: Friston's
  action variable `a` acts directly on the equations of motion of hidden states (Eq. 7's `ẍ =
  f(x̃,ṽ,a,θ)+w̃`), not on a **discharged, edge-local disagreement** the way Patchworks' motor edge
  clears. Patchworks' lane vocabulary is a genuine architectural addition over Friston's
  scalar formulation, not a restatement of it.
- The spec's "historically shakier for anything needing lookahead" (ADR-0003, known exposure) is
  correct and specifically attested: the field's own solution to the gap was not to extend the
  reflexive/continuous formulation but to bolt on a structurally different discrete POMDP layer with
  expected free energy over policies (see next section) — which is exactly the "second mode of
  operation in which the agent simulates without acting" that ADR-0003 refuses. The spec's bet is
  therefore not merely fragile in the abstract; it is betting against the field's own documented
  response to the same problem.

## 2. Planning without rollout

### Source

- Rao, R.P.N., Gklezakos, D.C., Sathish, V. (2022/2024). "Active Predictive Coding..." As above,
  §6 "Application to Hierarchical Planning" and §6.2 "Results," read in full from the arXiv PDF
  (v1, 2210.13461).
- Da Costa, L., Parr, T., Sajid, N., Veselic, S., Neacsu, V., Friston, K. (2020). "Active inference
  on discrete state-spaces: a synthesis." arXiv:2001.07203 (HTML rendering read in full).
- Friston, K., FitzGerald, T., Rigoli, F., Schwartenbeck, P., Pezzulo, G. (2017). "Active
  Inference: A Process Theory." *Neural Computation* 29(1), 1–49. (PDF located; text not cleanly
  extractable — see gaps; findings below sourced from Da Costa et al. 2020, which restates the same
  formalism with citable equations.)

### Findings

**Rao's APC plans by explicit rollout, not clamp propagation.** §6.2: "random-sampling shooting
model-predictive control (MPC): random state-action trajectories of length 4 were generated using
`Fs` by starting from the current state and picking one of the four random actions `Ai` for each
next state; the action sequence with the highest total reward was selected and its first action was
executed." Figure 9's caption confirms the mechanism samples `N` candidate high-level state-action
trajectories and picks the best by total reward before acting. This is genuine simulation of
counterfactual futures — the literal thing ADR-0003 refuses ("no second mode of operation in which
the agent simulates without acting").

**Standard discrete active inference's expected free energy (EFE) over policies has the same
shape.** Da Costa et al. (2020), Eq. 10: `G(π) = D_KL[Q(s_τ,A|π)‖P(s_τ,A)] −
𝔼_{Q(s_τ,A|π)P(o_τ|s_τ,A)}[log P(o_τ|s_τ,A)]` — risk plus ambiguity, one value per candidate
policy `π` (a full future action sequence). Policy selection is `Q(π) = σ(−G(π))` (Eq. 10), a
softmax over the *set* of evaluated policies. The paper states outright: "planning (i.e., computing
the expected free energy) for each possible policy can be cost-prohibitive, due to the combinatorial
explosion in the number of sequences of actions when looking deep into the future" — i.e., the
standard formulation is explicitly a search over a policy space, whose only documented problem is
that it does not scale, not that it avoids evaluating multiple candidates. Epistemic value enters
as its own term inside `G(π)` (Eq. 14, expected information gain,
`𝔼_Q[D_KL[Q(s_τ,A|o_τ,π)‖Q(s_τ,A|π)]]`), evaluated per policy, before any policy is executed.

**No primary source was found in which a predictive-coding/active-inference system achieves
genuine multi-step planning by pure forward propagation of a persistent clamp, with no rollout
mode at all.** Every source examined that plans past one reflexive step — Rao's APC, and
discrete/EFE active inference as formalized by Da Costa et al. and (per the 2310.05144 survey)
Parr, Pezzulo & Friston's monograph — does so by evaluating multiple candidate futures and choosing
among them. The Friston et al. (2010) continuous formulation this spec's identity is drawn from
(§1 above) is rollout-free, but is also not a planning system in any of these papers' sense — it
has no policy variable and no comparison step at all, only a single standing attractor a gradient
descends toward.

### Correspondences

- **This is a genuine and direct tension with the spec's "planning is clamp propagation" and
  ADR-0003's "no counterfactual evaluation at all."** The literature's actual planning mechanism,
  wherever planning exists at all (Rao's APC, discrete EFE active inference), is rollout: multiple
  candidate futures scored and compared. Patchworks' candidate answer — "several clamps propagating
  from different abstract regions, colliding, and reconciling" — has no attested precedent as a
  substitute for rollout in any source examined. This is not evidence the candidate answer is wrong;
  it is evidence that no one has published the thing Patchworks proposes to try instead, which is
  exactly what the spec itself already says (route selection, [#25], "genuinely promising and
  genuinely unproven").
- This item and the headline verdict are the same underlying finding wearing two names: Rao's paired
  state/action networks (headline) and Rao's explicit-rollout planning (this item) are two aspects
  of one architectural commitment the spec declines to make. Both are correctly pre-flagged by the
  spec's own "Known exposure" list and by [route selection, #25] — this pass adds primary-source
  weight to an exposure the spec had already priced in, rather than surfacing a new one.

## 3. Efference copy as an error channel

### Source

- von Holst, E., Mittelstaedt, H. (1950). "Das Reafferenzprinzip." *Naturwissenschaften* 37,
  464–476. **Not read in the original German**; characterized here from secondary description only
  (Wikipedia, Fandom "Reafference principle," and a ResearchGate figure reproduction of the
  original schematic) — the founding statement of "efference copy," but not verified against
  primary text. Flagged as **cited second-hand only**.
- Adams, R.A., Shipp, S., Friston, K.J. (2013). "Predictions not commands: active inference in the
  motor system." *Brain Structure and Function* 218(3), 611–643. **Full text could not be
  retrieved** — Springer's page requires institutional authentication (redirected to an IDP login),
  ResearchGate returned HTTP 403, and no open-access PDF mirror could be located (fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk
  does not host it under any filename tried). Findings below are drawn from search-engine-indexed
  summaries of the abstract and are flagged accordingly — **abstract-level only, body not read.**

### Findings (as far as verifiable)

The reafference principle's original move: an efferent (outgoing motor) signal generates a copy
(the "efference copy") that is compared against the actual reafferent sensory signal produced by
the movement; a match cancels self-generated sensory change (explaining stable visual perception
during eye movements), a mismatch signals the movement did not do what was commanded. Structurally,
this is a **comparison/error mechanism between a command-copy and an outcome** — it is a discrepancy
signal by original construction, not merely a record.

Adams, Shipp & Friston (2013), per the retrievable summary: active inference dispenses with the
classical inverse-model "motor command" altogether — descending signals from cortex are
proprioceptive **predictions**, and movement occurs by low-level reflex arcs discharging those
predictions (consistent with §1 above), rather than commands with a comparator efference copy
downstream. The retrievable summary states active inference's forward/generative model "generates
both proprioceptive and sensory predictions, and an inverse mapping converts a proprioceptive
prediction into movement" — no separate efference-copy comparator is described as a distinct
component; the comparison work is folded into the same predictive-coding hierarchy already doing
perception.

### Correspondences

- CONTEXT.md's **efference copy**: "what an actuator boundary cell writes back after the world has
  read it: the command as actually applied, post-clip and post-saturation... makes a motor edge
  carry ordinary disagreement rather than being the one edge with none." This is closer to von
  Holst & Mittelstaedt's original than to Adams/Shipp/Friston's active-inference reworking, on the
  point that matters: Patchworks' efference copy is explicitly a **discrepancy-producing signal**
  compared against the outgoing command to yield disagreement — the same comparator role von Holst
  & Mittelstaedt gave it in 1950. Active inference's own descendant move (per the retrievable
  summary) folds the comparison into the predictive hierarchy itself rather than keeping a
  standalone "actual-vs-commanded" comparator as a named object — closer in spirit to Patchworks'
  refusal of a transported error channel (see the tick-semantics pass's Rao & Ballard divergence)
  than to a literal instantiation of Patchworks' efference-copy mechanism.
- No contradiction found. Patchworks' efference copy sits at neither pole exactly: it keeps von
  Holst & Mittelstaedt's *object* (a copy of the applied command, compared against something) while
  routing the comparison through the same disagreement machinery every other edge uses, which is
  closer to Adams/Shipp/Friston's *unification* move (no separate comparator architecture) than to
  the classical forward-model picture. This correspondence should be read with real caution — it
  rests on the primary reafference paper being read second-hand and on Adams/Shipp/Friston being
  read at abstract level only, not on the source's own equations or evidence.

## 4. Retinotopic tiling as input, versus a learned encoder

### Source

- Rao, R.P.N., Ballard, D.H. (1999). "Predictive coding in the visual cortex..." *Nature
  Neuroscience* 2(1), 79–87. Re-cited from the tick-semantics citation pass
  (`docs/research/019-tick-semantics-citations.md`), which read the paper's Methods directly; not
  re-fetched independently for this pass (Nature's page requires institutional login and no open
  mirror was located this time either) — **findings below are carried over from a prior verified
  primary-source read, not freshly re-verified.**

### Findings

Per the prior pass's direct reading of the Methods: the model is organized as many small local
modules, each with its own causes vector `r` and basis `U`, operating over a fixed-size image patch
(32 level-1 causes over a 256-pixel, i.e. 16×16, patch in their simulations); a level-2 module then
combines the outputs of **three separate level-1 modules**, each anchored to its own patch, into
128 level-2 causes. This is a genuinely modular, patch-tiled architecture at small scale — the
paper does not use one global encoder compressing a whole image into one representation; it uses
many local predictors, each responsible for its own patch, feeding a combining level above them.

No claim in the paper's Methods (as previously read) states or implies that retinotopy is
*designed in* beyond the choice to tile by patch — spatial adjacency between patches is what
carries the retinotopic structure, exactly as Patchworks claims for its own tiling ("retinotopy is
then not designed; it falls out, because adjacent patches are adjacent cells").

### Correspondences

- **Direct precedent, at much smaller scale.** Rao & Ballard's three-patches-into-one-level-2-module
  structure is the same shape as Patchworks' "sensory input arrives sliced across many boundary
  cells, each owning a patch" — both refuse a single compressed global encoder in favor of many
  local units whose adjacency (not any designed coordinate) carries retinotopic structure. Patchworks
  scales this from 3 level-1 modules to a 64×64 tiling, which is a difference of degree the founding
  paper does not itself test, but the architectural principle — many local predictors over patches,
  not one global encoder — is the same principle, independently arrived at, as documented in the
  019 pass's direct reading of Eq. 1, Eq. 3, and Fig. 1b.
- No divergence found on this specific question. (The 019 pass's own flagged divergence — Rao &
  Ballard's error being a transported, first-class neural signal, versus Patchworks' derived-never-
  transported disagreement — is a different axis, already documented there, and not re-litigated
  here.)

## 5. The dark-room problem and epistemic drive

### Source

- Friston, K., Thornton, C., Clark, A. (2012). "Free-energy minimization and the dark-room
  problem." *Frontiers in Psychology* 3:130. Full text read (frontiersin.org).
- Da Costa, L. et al. (2020), as above — for the *later*, formal treatment of epistemic value as a
  term inside expected free energy.

### Findings

**The 2012 paper's own resolution does not invoke a separate epistemic-value term at all** — it
resolves the puzzle definitionally, not mechanistically. Its core move distinguishes `H(S)`, the
entropy of sensory states alone (which a literal dark room does minimize), from `H(S|m)`, entropy
conditioned on the agent's model `m` — "surprise... depends on sensations *and the agent (model)
predicting them*." Their conclusion: "a dark room will afford low levels of surprise if, and only
if, the agent has been optimized... to predict and inhabit it" — for any agent whose priors/model
were shaped (by evolution or development) to expect a rich, structured environment, sitting in a
dark room is *itself* highly surprising relative to that model, so free-energy minimization
predicts exploration, not retreat. No exploration bonus, curiosity term, or information-gain
quantity appears anywhere in this paper's text; the resolution is entirely in the relationship
between an agent's priors and its niche.

**The now-standard "epistemic value as an explicit term in expected free energy" resolution is a
later, separate development**, not this paper's own argument. Da Costa et al. (2020) formalizes it
as the second summand of `G(π)` (their Eq. 14, expected information gain — see item 2 above),
computed and traded off against pragmatic (reward-seeking) value per candidate policy before
acting.

### Correspondences

- Patchworks' answer — "whatever drive eventually supplies curiosity... enters as an ordinary clamp
  on an ordinary cell, not as a new channel and not as a second error signal" — is compatible with
  *both* documented resolutions, on different grounds. Against the **2012 paper's own argument**,
  Patchworks' answer is arguably the more literal one: if the fix is "shape the model/priors
  correctly," a clamp on an ordinary cell using the ordinary cell contract is exactly a prior shaping
  move, with no new mechanism — closer to Friston/Thornton/Clark's actual 2012 resolution than the
  standard textbook gloss ("active inference solves the dark room with an epistemic-value term") that
  is often attributed to this paper but does not appear in it.
- Against the **later formal EFE resolution** (Da Costa et al.), Patchworks' answer is a genuine and
  named divergence, in the same family as item 2: the field's mainstream mechanism is a distinct,
  explicitly evaluated term inside a per-policy objective, computed before acting; Patchworks has no
  policy objective at all and proposes to fold curiosity into the same clamp-and-propagate machinery
  used for goals. This is not shown to be wrong by either source, but it is shown to be the harder
  path, structurally consistent with item 2's rollout finding rather than an independent new
  exposure.

## 6. Thalamic relay / broadcast subspaces and TRN-style gating

### Source

- Sherman, S.M. (2005). "The role of the thalamus in cortical function: not just a simple relay."
  *Thalamus & Related Systems* 3(3), 205–216. Read in full (PDF).
- Crick, F. (1984). "Function of the thalamic reticular complex: the searchlight hypothesis." *PNAS*
  81(14), 4586–4590. **Not read in full** — PNAS's PDF returned HTTP 403 and no open mirror was
  located; findings below are from the abstract and secondary description only, flagged accordingly.

### Findings

Sherman (2005) distinguishes two relay types, both first-class thalamic mechanisms rather than
"preprocessing" in any deprecating sense: **first-order relays** (e.g., the lateral geniculate
nucleus) transmit ascending subcortical (e.g. retinal) input to cortex; **higher-order relays**
(e.g., the pulvinar) transmit information "from layer 5 of one cortical area to another" —
i.e., cortex-to-cortex communication routed *through* the thalamus rather than over direct
corticocortical axons. Verbatim (abstract): "Higher order relays seem to be important to
corticocortical communication, which challenges the dogma that such communication is based on
direct cortico-cortical connections." Table 1 (p. 207) formally distinguishes **driver** inputs
(which "determine relay cell receptive field") from **modulator** inputs (which do not) — the
thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN) is classified as a modulator, not a driver: it does not carry the
primary information being relayed, it gates/shapes how the driver signal is transmitted (GABAergic,
metabotropic-receptor-activating, per Table 1's "Modulator: TRN & Int" column).

Crick (1984), per the retrievable abstract: proposes the TRN implements an "attentional
searchlight" — bursts of TRN firing gate which thalamic relay cells transmit, implementing a
selection/attention mechanism external to the relay cells' own driving input.

### Correspondences

- **Direct, well-supported precedent for "a thalamic-style unification of the visual edge is a
  relay cell, not preprocessing."** Sherman's higher-order relay is precisely a **shared
  broadcast/relay subspace between distant regions** that is itself a first-class, costed
  neurobiological structure (synapses, driver/modulator distinction, its own circuitry) — not a
  transparent wire and not something "outside" the system doing free work before information
  reaches the graph proper. This directly supports the spec's membership rule: "every
  transformation of information on the way in belongs [to the graph], including relay stopovers and
  shared broadcast subspaces... it costs a tick and obeys the contract" — Sherman's higher-order
  relay literally costs synaptic and (relative to direct corticocortical transmission) topological
  overhead, and it is not classified as pre-cortical preprocessing by the source; it is
  corticocortical machinery of the same general kind as what it connects.
- **Partial tension, worth naming precisely.** CONTEXT.md's **relay cell** is "a cell whose
  inference is the identity: it holds stalks and restriction maps but performs no prediction" — a
  pure pass-through. Sherman's higher-order relay is *not* identity-like in this strict sense: it is
  gated and modulated (by TRN, by layer-6 corticothalamic feedback, by brainstem inputs) rather than
  transmitting its driver input unchanged. The correspondence Patchworks needs is with the **driver**
  pathway specifically (which does determine relay-cell receptive field one-for-one, closer to
  identity), not with the relay nucleus as a whole, which is a gated, modulatable object in the
  source. This is a precision gap in the spec's analogy, not a contradiction — the spec's own
  §"membership rule" already treats any nontrivial transformation as belonging to the graph proper
  (i.e., as a predicting cell, not a relay cell), so a TRN-gated thalamic relay would, if modelled at
  this level of fidelity, likely correspond to a predicting cell doing gating rather than to
  Patchworks' strict relay cell — worth a documentation note if this ever needs unpacking further,
  not a revision.

## 7. Saccades, rejected

### Source

- Friston, K., Adams, R.A., Perrinet, L., Breakspear, M. (2012). "Perceptions as hypotheses:
  saccades as experiments." *Frontiers in Psychology* 3:151. Read via PMC (PMC3361132).
- Rao, Gklezakos & Sathish (2022/2024), as above, §5.1 and Supplementary §1.1.1 — read in full.

### Findings

**Friston, Adams, Perrinet & Breakspear (2012).** Saccades are modelled as **active sampling**
driven by expected information gain: "salience is the precision of counterfactual beliefs that
depend on where or how sensory data are sampled" — a foveal viewport is fixated wherever it is
expected to most reduce uncertainty about hidden causes. The model's own restriction to a moving,
size-limited high-resolution sample ("a 16×16 sampling grid" as an "attentional focus," modelling
"the loss of precise visual information from the periphery") is doing real theoretical work in this
paper: it is the vehicle for the epistemic-value computation itself, and the paper frames solving
for "fictive hidden states in the future" as "relatively simple" precisely *because* the viewport is
small and discrete. The paper does not discuss, let alone justify against, an alternative in which
the whole visual field is available at once as many parallel channels — that comparison is simply
outside its scope.

**Rao's APC, §5.1.** Confirms the same design choice independently: "the actions in the APC model
emulate eye movements (or 'attention') and correspond to moving a 'glimpse sensor'... which extracts
high-resolution information about a small part of a larger input image." Supplementary §1.1.1 gives
exact numbers: a 14×14 top-level reference frame and 7×7 bottom-level glimpses over a 28×28
(MNIST/FMNIST) or 32×32 (Omniglot) image — a small fraction of the field visible at any moment, by
construction.

### Correspondences

- Both sources buy the **same** thing with a restricted viewport: a small, information-gain-driven
  glimpse makes the search over "where to sample next" tractable, and (in Rao's case specifically)
  lets one small state/action network pair be reused at every fixation rather than needing a
  network sized to the whole field. Neither paper states or measures what is lost by removing the
  restriction; both simply build the restriction in as a design starting point, motivated by
  computational tractability and biological verisimilitude to actual saccadic vision — not
  defended against a whole-field alternative.
- The spec's *Considered and rejected* note is therefore accurate as far as it goes ("restricting
  the agent to a small moving viewport, as the active predictive coding papers do") but slightly
  understates how load-bearing the restriction is in both sources: it is not an incidental choice
  either paper could straightforwardly drop, since Friston et al.'s epistemic-value machinery is
  defined *in terms of* choosing where to sample next, and Rao's parameter-sharing argument (§6,
  "the same policy can be re-used at multiple locations") depends on the field being small enough
  that one glimpse network's local reference frame generalizes across the image. Patchworks' choice
  to make the whole field visible as many boundary cells sidesteps needing that specific
  tractability trick because it does not amortize one small network across many locations in the
  first place — every patch already has its own cell. This is a real, if underlined-in-passing,
  correspondence in Patchworks' favor: the architectural feature that made saccades necessary for
  APC/active-inference vision (parameter sharing across a whole field via one small reused network)
  is exactly the feature Patchworks' per-patch boundary-cell tiling does not have, so there is
  nothing in these two sources that argues Patchworks *needs* saccades once it has abandoned network
  reuse across the visual field. Consistent with, and slightly strengthens, the spec's rejection.

## Verdict

The design survives the pass on its own stated terms. Its highest-fragility bet (§ Headline
verdict) is **mixed rather than cleanly won or lost**: strongly supported by the free-energy
mathematics it is drawn from, and directly undercut by the specifically-named framework it opens by
invoking. Two further items (planning without rollout; the dark-room resolution) inherit the same
tension in weaker form. Nothing found in this pass contradicts a claim the spec makes about itself
with the force the sandbox pass's `armature` bug or friction-square findings did (see
`018-sandbox-citations.md`) — every tension here is a tension between the spec and the wider
literature's dominant approach, which the spec already names and pre-accepts as its "APC bet" and
its "no plan comparison" consequence.

### Revision tickets warranted

None. Every finding in this pass falls inside a risk ADR-0003 and the spec's own "Known exposure"
section already named and pre-accepted ("a revision sweep through this section and its dependents
is pre-accepted" if the bet fails outright — it has not failed outright; it has been shown to be a
real, live bet rather than a free one). Per the map's Notes, this pass validates after the fact and
does not itself trigger a revision sweep.

### Documentation-only (worth a line, not a redesign)

1. **The spec's opening move ("active predictive coding has an upstream and a downstream... one
   algorithm running in opposite directions") reads as if it describes Rao's Active Predictive
   Coding paper, and it does not** — that paper is paired-network and rollout-planning at every
   level it has (Headline verdict; item 2). The spec's actual mathematical grounding is Friston et
   al. (2010)'s free-energy formulation, which *does* support the directional reading. A precise
   reader could reasonably think the spec is claiming to unify Rao's specific architecture; it is
   really claiming to unify Friston's more general one. Worth a sentence distinguishing "active
   predictive coding" as invoked (the general free-energy-driven perception/action unification) from
   Rao et al.'s paper of that exact name (a specific, structurally two-sided instance of it) — the
   spec currently lets a reader conflate the two.
2. **"Planning without rollout" and "epistemic value without a new channel" are, so far, unattested
   in the literature as stated** (items 2 and 5). This is not a defect — the spec already frames
   route selection ([#25]) as "genuinely promising and genuinely unproven" — but this pass found
   zero positive precedent in either direction, only that the field's actual solutions both do the
   thing Patchworks refuses (rollout; a distinct epistemic-value term). Worth noting in the same
   register as the spec's existing "Known exposure" list, if it is not already read that way.
3. **The relay-cell / higher-order-thalamic-relay correspondence needs one qualifier** (item 6):
   Sherman's higher-order relay is gated/modulated (by TRN and other modulator inputs), not a pure
   identity pass-through — closer to a predicting cell doing gating than to CONTEXT.md's strict
   relay cell. Does not conflict with anything the spec claims (the spec's own membership rule
   already routes any nontrivial transformation to the graph proper), but the specific word "relay
   cell" used in the spec's example sentence is a slightly loose fit to the source it evokes.

  accordingly.

  summary page, and a ResearchGate figure reproduction of the original schematic), not the source
  text itself.

  Theory," was located but its PDF could not be reliably parsed** (heavily compressed/encoded
  stream); its findings are covered instead via Da Costa et al. (2020)'s citable restatement of the
  same discrete/EFE formalism with explicit equations, which is treated as the primary source for
  item 2 and item 5's later-development claim.

  expected-free-energy-over-policies formalism; this pass relies on that survey's characterization
  rather than the monograph's own text.
